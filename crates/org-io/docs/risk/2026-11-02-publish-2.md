# org-io: risks of the removed-Devices query and the dial by key

Change `worktree-org-io-publish-2`, task T13b (§0, owner rulings R1–R4 of
2026-11-02), analysed 2026-11-03 (`analyze-risks`) for
`crates/org-io/docs/requirements/2026-11-02-publish-2.md`. The owner was not
interviewed in this task: the assessments are the analyst's, and the owner
accepted them as drafted on 2026-11-03 (*owner ruling Q5*). No new hazard is minted.

## Derived requirements assessment

### The removed-Devices query (REQ-xghnh3)

It passes org-node's public values to the app (MemberIds,
DevicePublicKeys, an epoch) and changes nothing. No key, seed, secret or
address crosses the boundary (the app's no-secret rule is unaffected). The risk of the Admin misreading an acknowledgement as proof
of erasure is the app's, analysed there (app
`2026-11-02-publish-2.md`). No hazard impact.

assesses: REQ-xghnh3

### The dial by key in Networked mode (REQ-kn8zg6, owner ruling Q1 (c) of 2026-11-03)

org-node's hazard of retained removal state (the state a revoked Device keeps to send its
acknowledgement outlives everything else on a lost or stolen Device): the
dial goes only to a recipient org-node already keeps, under the Persona's
own key, within the same 7 days (REQ-98a3ty); it keeps nothing new, so the
retained state is unchanged. The dial by key reveals to iroh's discovery and
relay service which DevicePublicKey this endpoint looks for, as every
Networked send already does (LLR-34tfm8); no other Device forwards the
acknowledgement (ruling D7). A Member still listing the Device refuses as
before (org-node's acknowledgement check, its sender clause), so a dial by key cannot get an
acknowledgement accepted that an addressed dial would not. Benefit side: the
delivering Device, and any recipient whose address was never kept, can be
reached in Networked mode, so more acknowledgements arrive within the 7
days. No new hazard; no rating changes.

Residual: in Loopback mode a recipient with no kept address stays
undelivered (`NoAddress`). Loopback is the single-host development mode;
the residual costs at most one undelivered acknowledgement there.

*Note 2026-11-04 (change `worktree-org-io-publish-2`, task q1r; owner
ruling Q1' of 2026-11-04; analyst's assessment, not interviewed).* The dial
by key now goes from an endpoint of the removed Device's identity bound with
iroh's relay server and address lookup (LLR-spckm6; in production
`presets::N0`: n0's relay servers, its DNS lookup and its Pkarr publisher),
because the direct-only bind could never find the key. What that service
learns, beyond the sentence above:

- *The removed Device's key.* The endpoint authenticates to the relay server
  under the removed Device's key, from the removed Device's IP address, and
  the Pkarr publisher publishes that key with its relay URL. Under
  the direct-only bind it replaces, a removed Device showed itself to no relay or lookup after its
  removal; now it does, for the life of one endpoint: bound for one dial
  and closed once its outcome is read (at most the dial's timeout plus the
  10 s verdict bound), only for a recipient with no usable address, only in
  Networked mode. Its Device secret never leaves it (iroh's handshake), and
  no accept loop runs on that endpoint, so nothing is received on it.
- *The recipient's key.* The lookup is asked for the recipient's key and the
  relay forwards packets from the removed Device's key to it, so the service
  learns the pair. Every Networked send already shows it the pair of sender
  and recipient (LLR-34tfm8), so it learned this pair while the Device was a
  Member; the recipient's endpoint is published under N0 already.
- *Timing.* The pair recurs on REQ-jqmpz5's schedule (T, T+1, T+5, T+30 min,
  then hourly) until the recipient accepts or the 7 days pass. That pattern
  lets the service operator infer that the Device is acting on a removal
  and which Device it reports to, which no send of a Member shows.

It learns no Organisation data, no address book entry beyond the
recipient's key, and nothing of the acknowledgement: the QUIC session is
end to end between the two Device keys, so the relay can neither read nor
alter it, and a relay that drops it costs at most an undelivered
acknowledgement, as `NoAddress` did. No other Device forwards it (ruling
D7). Effect on ratings: org-node's hazard of retained removal state is an
exposure to whoever holds the lost or stolen Device, and what the Device
keeps is unchanged, so its rating stands; HAZ-abm3xs's inbound side already accepts relayed connections
(RC-6m89tr). No hazard in org-io or org-node rates what iroh's relay or
lookup operator observes; the metadata above is of the kind Networked mode
already shows it, with the removal's timing pattern added. No new hazard; no
rating changes. For the owner's attention, not a pending ruling: the
removal's timing pattern is the one new observation, and it is visible only
to the operator of the relay and lookup service the deployment chooses.

*Ruled 2026-11-08 (owner).* The relay and lookup operator's view of a dial
by key stays a risk note, not a hazard: no new hazard is minted and no
rating changes. To be revisited when self-hosted relays are planned.

assesses: REQ-kn8zg6

## The own-admin check retired (owner ruling 6; plan task T16h)

The own-admin check's requirement is retired, deleted with its low-level
requirements; nothing called the check
outside org-io's own tests, and the app never did (the app's invitation-warning control, its note of

assessments of the check's two requirements are deleted with them. No
hazard impact.
