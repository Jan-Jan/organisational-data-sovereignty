# org-io: risk assessment of the publish path after a write (S3b-io, part 1)

Change `worktree-org-io-publish`. Drafted 2026-10-08 with
`org-io/docs/requirements/2026-10-13-publish.md`. Judged
against this unit's acceptability matrix (`README.md`): S3 is unacceptable
at every probability (ruling D, 2026-10-06), so no S3 residual here is
called acceptable under the matrix. No new hazard is identified for part 1;
the four requirements below narrow hazards analysed in org-node's register
(`org-node/docs/risk/2026-09-09-org-node-hazards.md`: the
publish-before-persist hazard, S3/P2) and in its commit-workflow analysis
(`org-node/docs/risk/2026-10-07-commit-workflow.md`: deletion on a forged or
misread chain state). They are named by description: org-node exports no
item. Part 2 (revocation delivery and acknowledgements) adds org-io's own
hazard analysis, including org-io's control for the retained acknowledgement
state, when it mints its requirements.

## Derived requirements

### REQ-by9k5q (a write whose outcome is unknown)

Mitigates the publish-before-persist hazard in its remaining form: a write
that executed after org-io stopped waiting leaves the chain an epoch ahead of
the record, and every later write is refused by the contract's epoch check,
so an administrator cannot act at all, the ordinary case being a revocation
of a stolen Device. Settling the unknown write by reading the chain and
committing the kept update removes that lock-out without a second write.
Failure behaviour: the requirement never commits on the update's own claim
(the commit is org-node's, judged against the chain state org-io read under
REQ-tg9zrn), never writes twice for one Organisation while the first is
unknown (and the contract's epoch check refuses a second write that races a
first after a restart), and sends the late commit only to the Device the
submission named, so no recipient receives an update meant for another
(the app's report on kept provisional updates). Owner ruling D3 (2026-10-08) removes the residual this text
recorded: a discard is refused until a finalised block past the write's
mortality shows the record's epoch, so a discarded update's write can no
longer execute. As recorded before the ruling: a discard while the write may
still execute leaves the node behind the chain if it then executes; the
node is then in org-node's "behind" state, refused nothing it holds and
deleting nothing, until a peer in good standing can supply the update
(stage S6). Availability only; no disclosure or retention follows.

*Updated 2026-10-14 (master's S3b-io part 1 merged into S4,
`worktree-org-io-transport`; re-assessed, no new hazard, no rating
change).* "Sends the late commit only to the Device the submission named"
reads, on the merged tree, "sends the late commit as its own submission
would have": S4's fan-out sends every body org-node's commit of that update
built to its own recipient, at the address that submission was given (the
clarification under REQ-by9k5q). Each body is the one org-node built for
that recipient from that update's commit, so no recipient receives an
update meant for another, and a later call's recipient receives nothing of
it: the control holds. The failure behaviour on a store write that fails is
stronger than before: the Persona is reloaded from its file, the unknown
write is kept, and the next settle or reconcile commits and sends again (S4,
task T9), where on S2's org-node the send could be lost (org-node's
reconcile-save report).

*Updated 2026-10-13 (review round 2, finding-1).* "A discard is refused
until a finalised block past the write's mortality shows the record's epoch"
now has one exception, REQ-by9k5q's case (b): a record already past the
epoch the write expected settles the write as not executed at once, inside
the window. The control D3 asked for is unchanged in effect: the write can
never execute, because the contract's epoch check refuses it at any epoch
but the one it expected, and the chain is at least at the record's; and the
commit that moved the record had already dropped the update with its
Organisation private key (org-node drops the orphans with every commit), so
the settle's discard removes nothing. No new residual.

assesses: REQ-by9k5q

### REQ-77rh5a (startup reconcile and the unverified state)

Mitigates the same hazard for a crash between the write and the commit: the
kept provisional update is committed from the chain's state at the next
start. Fails closed when the chain cannot be read: the last verified record
is kept, marked unverified, and nothing is committed or deleted (owner
ruling of 2026-10-06), so a transient RPC fault never reaches org-node as a
verdict. A behind or refused result changes nothing. The report to the app
carries public data only (epochs, roots, Device public keys, reasons): no
private key reaches the app (REQ-v4tfap). Residual: an unverified
Organisation keeps working on a record that may be stale; its users see it
marked unverified, which is the ruled trade-off. In a release build every
Organisation is unverified until S8 (REQ-8zuka3).

assesses: REQ-77rh5a

### REQ-dqbg9p (discard, in the background)

*Re-assessed 2026-10-13 on the owner's ruling of that day (the discard is
org-io's own; the app's discard commands are removed). Hazard pass for the
removal of the user's manual discard: a reduction.* The manual discard could
remove the very update a write was about to put on the chain, or one whose
write might still execute (a refusal guarded the first, ruling D3 the
second); with no user-facing discard, neither route exists, and a user can
no longer discard by mistake the update that would have committed. What
remains is automatic and narrower: on a commit, the updates not built on the
new root — which can never commit, because the contract's epoch check and
org-node's base-root match both refuse them — are dropped with their keys;
on a founding, the Persona's other genesis updates, which `commit_genesis`
would refuse for a bound Persona anyway; and an update whose write is known
not to have executed. Each removes an Organisation private key that serves
no purpose, which narrows the store's key exposure (org-node's encrypted
store analysis). It touches no record and no expectation, so it cannot undo
a commit. Residual: a Persona whose foundings all failed keeps their genesis
updates, with their keys in the encrypted store, until one of its foundings
commits — *accepted by the owner on 2026-10-13 only until a feature that
lands after S4*: the user may delete a Persona that is not yet on chain
(its membership proposal still awaiting admission, or its founding's write
failed or never made), which removes its leftover genesis updates; while a
founding's write outcome is unknown, the deletion waits until the 64-block
window has passed and a finalised block confirms the write did not land
(ruling D3) (roadmap row "S4b"); and a founding that timed out and executed
later than a successful one leaves an on-chain slot no node holds the key
of. Neither discloses or retains membership data.

*Re-assessed 2026-10-13 (review round 1, finding-2; owner ruling of that
day).* The founding residual contradicted the requirement's "never discard
an update whose write may still execute", which is now narrowed to update
writes. The owner ruled it acceptable: "Considering we are using a proxy to
write to chain for organisations, the founding of an org will create their
own proxy. I don't think this is a real concern in practice, but the tests
must cover that a new proxy is created upon creating/founding a new org."
Every founding creates its own new pure proxy (on-chain-client's genesis
ceremony, now tested for two foundings yielding two distinct proxies), so a
timed-out founding that lands after its genesis update was dropped founds a
separate Organisation at another H160: it cannot move, overwrite or collide
with the committed Organisation's slot. The orphaned slot holds a public
root and public key only and costs its funding deposit; availability and
cost only, no disclosure or retention. Accepted.

assesses: REQ-dqbg9p

### REQ-cz753a (no deletion on any other trigger)

Mitigates deletion on a misread chain (org-node's commit-workflow analysis:
a device deletes everything only after verifying the revocation against the
chain, from a peer it still holds as a member; owner ruling R3 of
2026-10-07, "the code should ideally make this failure if not impossible
then extremely unlikely"). org-io adds no deletion path of its own in part
1, and the requirement makes that checkable: a source scan for removal
operations and behavioural tests on each non-verified trigger (failed read,
refused parse, behind, stale, conflict, unknown write, discard, refused
message) that the store and the record are unchanged.

assesses: REQ-cz753a

### REQ-ntkjk6 (own-admin check, superseding REQ-f3eu9n; task T16a)

Narrows the misreport REQ-f3eu9n allowed (PR-wk7zwq): a delegate that the
chain would refuse to dispatch for — a restricted proxy type, or a delay,
`Unannounced` — was reported an admin. Now "admin" is exactly the definition
the writer's forced-`Any` dispatch uses, read with the mapped account at one
block (on-chain-client's two-block-read report), so no answer combines two chain states. A failed read is
still an error, never "not an admin". Its first caller (part 2's
limit-setting command) gates a local setting only; the chain still refuses
any write a non-admin attempts, so a wrong answer can at worst let a node
set a limit for its own device (D6) or refuse it to an admin. No new hazard.

assesses: REQ-ntkjk6
