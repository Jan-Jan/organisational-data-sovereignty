## SDD-6qz9ms — The chain read

`org-io/src/chain_read.rs`: the `StateReader` trait (org-io's own seam;
ruling B binds org-node's interface, not org-io's), `org_state_from_chain`
(moved from `org-node/src/chain_read.rs`), and the receive sequence in
`org-io/src/lib.rs` (`OrgIo::receive_and_verify`,
`OrgIo::receive_and_self_delete_if_revoked`). The production reader over
on-chain-client (`OnChainStateReader`) is the chain-connection item's.

**SDD-6qz9ms**: the read of an Organisation's state from the chain and its
hand-over to org-node as a value: org-io reads, parses through org-node's
Organisation-state parse, and passes the result, or its absence, to the
org-node operation that judges against it; on the receive path it reads only
after org-node's chain-free checks have accepted the message.
traces: REQ-tg9zrn

**LLR-7pj5af**: `OrgIo::receive_and_verify` and
`OrgIo::receive_and_self_delete_if_revoked` take one message and the
Device that sent it from org-node's `receive_message`, and hand both to
org-node's chain-free phase (`prepare_receive`, `prepare_self_delete`).
When that phase refuses, they return its error unchanged and make no
`StateReader::read_state` call; when it finishes without a chain state
(`Prepared::Done`: an acknowledgement, checked against the store alone),
they return its outcome and make no call; only when it returns a pending
value (`Prepared::NeedsChain`) do they call `read_state` exactly once,
for the Organisation the pending value names, then hand the state read
(or `None`) to org-node's apply phase (`apply_receive`,
`apply_self_delete`). When `read_state` fails, or the parse refuses the
state, they return that error and call no apply phase, so nothing is
committed or written. The handle offers no chain-judging org-node
operation (commit, reconcile, prepare/apply) outside this read-then-call
sequence: `node_mut()` hands out org-node's `NodeBuilders`, never
`&mut OrgService`.
satisfies: REQ-tg9zrn

*Clarified 2026-10-08 (review round 1, finding 5), in place with no new ID:
the last sentence of the item is added. It states explicitly what "only
when … do they call `read_state` … then hand the state read to org-node's
apply phase" already required: no path around that sequence. Verified by
`the_handle_offers_no_chain_judging_operation_outside_its_own_sequence`
(org-io/tests/absences.rs).*

*Narrowed 2026-10-08 (review round 2, finding 2).* The last sentence said
the handle offers no chain-judging operation "(commit, reconcile,
prepare/apply)" outside the sequence. That overclaimed for prepare:
`prepare_receive` and `prepare_self_delete` take `&self`, so `node()` and
`NodeBuilders`' `Deref` offer them, and they may be offered because they are
chain-free. The clause covers commit, reconcile and apply only. It also
did not hold for the app: a refused `OrgIo::connect` handed back a bare
`OrgService` (`ConnectFailed`'s public `service`), and the app built its own

under the hybrid rule: a narrowing (prepare leaves the clause's list; the
rest of its meaning is unchanged and now holds), so the ID is kept.
Verified by `org_io_hands_out_no_bare_service` (absences.rs) and
`the_app_calls_no_chain_judging_operation_and_builds_no_service`

### The item with no low-level requirements

SDD-z85ux9 carries none, under a class that requires them. This section is the
deviation, recorded here rather than left for a reader to notice.

**What it is not.** The `on-chain-client` unit has an item of the same shape —
its chain-facing transport shell, the one item its decomposition records as
carrying no low-level requirements, described in the section "The item with no
low-level requirements" of
`on-chain-client/docs/architecture/2026-09-28-decomposition.md` — and **its

at all. This one is exercised, by the chopsticks target `org-io/tests/preflight.rs`
(the chain.live check, stalled and mined), moved with it from org-node.

**What it is.** That target is excluded from this unit's `verify_commands`,
by a decision recorded in `org-io/.guardrails/config.yaml`: it spawns a
chopsticks fork and needs `on-chain/scripts/node_modules`, neither of which
the gate provides. So the honest statement is narrower than
on-chain-client's, and worse:

> A low-level requirement written here could be true, could be well-tested, and
> **no mutation to the code it describes would redden this unit's gate.** It
> would be an assertion carrying no evidence that this gate can produce.

That is the failure this repository has recorded more than once — a property
credited to an assertion that cannot observe it — and writing LLRs into the
one part of the unit where the gate is blind would be committing it
deliberately.

**What follows from it.** The remedy is not to write the LLRs. It is to bring
the chopsticks target into the gate, which is an infrastructure change of its
own — a hermetic chopsticks fixture, or a recorded-transcript substitute for
the RPC layer — booked in `docs/plans/2026-09-05-ratchet-setup.md` rather than
attempted here. What can be pinned without a chain is pinned: the read rule
(`read_org_state`, LLR-rm9x4z) is separated from `OnChainStateReader` by the
`RawStateSource` seam precisely so that it is gated.
