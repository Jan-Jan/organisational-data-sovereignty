`docs/plans/2026-10-06-org-io-roadmap.md`. Plan:
`docs/plans/2026-10-08-org-io-publish.md` (part 1, tasks T13a–T17a). Drafted
2026-10-08, before any code, from the proposed org-io requirements 2, 3, 4
and 10 of `docs/plans/2026-10-06-org-io-commit-workflow.md`, reconciled with
the merged S2 change (`e41cb09`). Until the part 1 tasks write their tests
each requirement below is reported MISSING-TEST, which is the truth.

Not minted here, and why:

- **Proposed 1, the order of the publish path**, is already this unit's:
  REQ-nfr3n2 with its low-level requirements (the write, then the read of
  the chain the write moved, then org-node's commit, then the send; the
  update written is the one org-node holds on the record's current root).
  S2 landed it. Nothing new is minted for it.
- **Proposed 5 to 9 and 11** (fan-out, revocation resend, receiving a
  revocation, sending and receiving acknowledgements, the Organisation's
  retry limits) wait for part 2. They depend on stage S4's keychain, store
  files, endpoints and acknowledgement inbound side, and on owner decisions
  D2, D6 and D7 of the plan. Part 2's first task mints them.

`org-io/docs/risk/2026-10-13-publish.md`. org-node's

## A write whose outcome is unknown

**REQ-by9k5q**: The software shall, when a chain write it submitted for an
Organisation's provisional update has not finished within its bound (its
outcome unknown), keep that provisional update and the Device the submission
named, submit no other chain write for that Organisation, and, before any
later submission for that Organisation and whenever the app asks, read the
Organisation's state from the chain: when the chain holds that update's
Membership root and Organisation public key, it shall have org-node commit
that update and send it to the Device the submission named; when the chain
is at a newer epoch with another root, it shall reconcile the Organisation
(REQ-77rh5a); when the chain is still at the record's epoch and a finalised
block later than the last block at which that write could be included has
been read, it shall report the write as not executed and end the unknown
state and discard the update (REQ-dqbg9p), after which another update may be
written; and otherwise, or when a read fails, it shall report the outcome as
still unknown.
satisfies: derived

*Amended in place 2026-10-08 (unmerged draft), owner ruling D3: the last
clause read "until one of the above holds or the app discards the update";
a discard now waits for the write's mortality to pass.* *Amended in place
2026-10-13 (owner ruling of that day, background discard): "after which the
update may be discarded (REQ-dqbg9p) or written again" became "and discard
the update (REQ-dqbg9p), after which another update may be written": the
discard is org-io's own, not the app's.*

*Note 2026-10-13 (unmerged draft; review round 2, finding-1):* a second
case reports the write as not executed. (b) When the record's epoch is
already past the epoch the write expected (a received update or a reconcile
moved it), the contract's epoch check means the write can never execute:
it is reported not executed at once, within its window and with no read,
and the unknown state ends. Owner ruling D3 still holds: it forbids
discarding an update whose write may still execute, and this one cannot;
and the commit that moved the record already dropped the update with every
other orphan of the Organisation (org-node's `discard_orphans`), so the
discard finds nothing left and changes nothing (LLR-t98pk9, case (b)).

*Corrected 2026-10-15 (S4 review round 2, A3; PR-9q5ems):* "the write can
never execute" holds, but "reported as not executed" does not follow from
the record's epoch alone: a record past the expected epoch may hold, or
follow, this write's own update. Case (b) reports not executed only when
the record is at the epoch after the expected one with another root and
Organisation public key; otherwise the chain is read and this item's main
clauses apply (LLR-t98pk9's note of the same day).

*Clarified 2026-10-14 (master's S3b-io part 1 merged into S4,
`worktree-org-io-transport`; hybrid rule, a clarification):* S4 changed
what a submission sends (its task T12: a committed update goes to every
recipient org-node built a body for, a failed send reported per Device), so
a submission names no single Device. On the merged tree "the Device the
submission named" reads "the recipients the submission would have sent
to": the late update is sent as its own submission would have sent it, at
the peer address that submission was given — its own Device among them, and
never the recipient of a later call, which is this item's point. What is
kept is the update and that address.

From proposed 2 (org-node's publish-before-persist problem report, and the
first half of the app's report on kept provisional updates; neither unit
exports its items to org-io, so both are named in the plan, not here). What it changes: S2
reports a timed-out write as "outcome unknown" and keeps the update, but
nothing ever settles it; the next admission is built on the record's root,
its write is refused by the contract's epoch check, and the node stays one
epoch behind the chain. "The Device the submission named" is that app
report's third clause: a write that executed late is sent to the Device it was
written for, never to the recipient of a later call. The unknown state is
held in memory only: after a restart the startup reconcile of REQ-77rh5a
settles it, and the contract's epoch check refuses a second write that
races the first. The last block of inclusion is known because every
update write is mortal from a finalised block read before it
(on-chain-client's mortal-write requirement, owner ruling D3).

*Clarified 2026-10-15 (S4 review round 2, finding-6; PR-k5tdxj):* "after a
restart the startup reconcile of REQ-77rh5a settles it" holds for the
record — the reconcile commits a write that executed, or finds the chain
elsewhere — but not for the send: after a restart no unknown write is held
in memory, so the reconcile that commits it sends its bodies to no one.
Delivering them is open under PR-k5tdxj (item 2) and S3b-io part 2.

## Startup reconcile and the unverified state

**REQ-77rh5a**: The software shall, at start for every Organisation it holds,
after a chain write whose outcome is unknown (REQ-by9k5q), and whenever the
app asks, read the Organisation's state from the chain, hand it to org-node's
reconcile, and report to the app exactly one result per Organisation: in
step; committed, with the chain's epoch and the Devices the committed update
is to be sent to; behind, with the chain's epoch, changing nothing; or
refused, with org-node's reason, changing nothing. When the read fails, or
the chain is not configured, it shall keep the Organisation's last verified
record, report the Organisation to the app as unverified with the reason,
commit and delete nothing for that Organisation, and reconcile it again when
the app asks.
satisfies: derived

From proposed 3 (the publish-before-persist report) and the owner's ruling of 2026-10-06 on a
device that cannot reach the chain (the old plan's decision 5). Changed from
the proposal on reconciliation with S2: the trigger "whenever a read shows
the chain at a newer epoch than a held record" is replaced by the unknown
write and the app's request, because the receive path reads a newer epoch
on every ordinary update and settles it itself; "on committed it fans the
update out" becomes "reports the Devices it is to be sent to", because the
fan-out is stage S4's (its requirement on Networked sends) and the send of a
reconciled commit is part 2's (owner ruling D1, 2026-10-08); "from S6,
asks a peer in good standing" is S6's and is not stated here. A release
build reads no chain until stage S8 (REQ-8zuka3), so every Organisation is
reported unverified there; that is the ruled availability limit, and it
fails closed.

## Discard, in the background

**REQ-dqbg9p**: The software shall discard provisional updates itself, with
no request from the app: when a provisional update for an Organisation is
committed — through its own submission, a settled late write, a reconcile
or a received update — every other provisional update held for that
Organisation; when a founding commits, every other genesis update the
founding Persona built; and an update whose write REQ-by9k5q has reported
not executed; each with the Organisation private key it holds. It shall
never discard an update whose write may still execute, and shall offer the
app no command that discards a provisional update.
satisfies: derived

*Rewritten in place 2026-10-13 (unmerged draft), owner ruling of
2026-10-13: "the discarding and accepting provisional updates happen in the
background (i.e. invisible to the user). Under normal circumstance there
should only be 1 provisional org information, but under adverse conditions
there could be multiple. Upon transitioning 1 provisional org information
to accepted all the other provisionals can be discarded. I.e. this should
happen in org-io."* The text before read: "The software shall offer the app
a command to discard a provisional update it names — an admission's or a
revocation's, by the name returned when it was built, or every genesis
update a named Persona built — which has org-node remove the update
together with the Organisation private key it holds; and shall refuse,
changing nothing, a request naming no update org-node holds, a request
naming an update whose chain write is in progress, and a request naming an
update whose write's outcome is unknown until REQ-by9k5q has reported that
write not executed." (amended 2026-10-08 with the unknown-outcome refusal,
owner ruling D3, which still holds as the "never … may still execute"
clause).

*Narrowed in place 2026-10-13 (unmerged draft; review round 1, finding-2,
owner ruling of that day):* "It shall never discard an update whose write
may still execute" applies to update writes — an admission's or a
revocation's, the writes REQ-by9k5q tracks — and not to genesis writes,
whose updates a founding's commit drops (LLR-36zp69) even when an earlier
founding of the Persona timed out and may still execute. The owner's
ruling: "Considering we are using a proxy to write to chain for
organisations, the founding of an org will create their own proxy. I don't
think this is a real concern in practice, but the tests must cover that a
new proxy is created upon creating/founding a new org." A timed-out
founding that lands later founds a separate Organisation under its own new
pure proxy, so it cannot collide with the committed one; its slot is one
that no node holds the key of, which is the accepted residual assessed in
the risk file. The test the ruling asks for is on-chain-client's
`write_compose.rs` `each_genesis_creates_a_new_pure_proxy_and_writes_through_it`
(its genesis-ceremony item).

From proposed 4 (the second half of the app's report on kept provisional
updates: "every failed founding leaves another genesis update holding an
Organisation private key, because no app command calls
`discard_provisional`"). The owner's ruling answers it without a command:
the commit that accepts one update drops the others. On a commit to an
Organisation, org-node already drops every provisional update for it not
built on the new root (its rule that every commit replaces the record and
discards the orphans), so org-io relies on that and verifies it through each
of its commit paths; on a founding, org-node drops the Persona's other
genesis updates (org-node's draft, task T15d). A Persona whose foundings all
failed keeps its genesis updates until one of its foundings commits; that
residual is assessed in the risk draft.

## No deletion on any other trigger

**REQ-cz753a**: The software shall delete no data it holds, or has org-node
hold, for an Organisation — a record, a Persona, a provisional update
other than one REQ-dqbg9p discards, a file, or a peer address — except when
org-node returns that deletion as its acceptance of a verified revocation or
its commit of a verified update that removes this node; a failed or refused
chain read, a chain state reported as behind, stale or in conflict, a write
whose outcome is unknown, and a refused message shall delete nothing else.
satisfies: derived

*Amended in place 2026-10-13 (unmerged draft; owner ruling of that day,
background discard): "a provisional update the app has not discarded" and
"a discard" in the trigger list became the discards REQ-dqbg9p makes
itself; the app discards nothing.*

*Clarified 2026-10-14 (master's S3b-io part 1 merged into S4; hybrid rule,
a clarification):* on S4's structure org-node returns a deletion as a store
change (`StoreChange::Forgotten`, only from a verified revocation's
acceptance or a commit that removes this node) and org-io executes it by
removing that Persona's store file (S4's LLR-amv4mb). That removal is
org-node's deletion on one of the two verified paths, so "org-io itself
deletes nothing" holds. A store write that fails makes org-io reload the
Persona from its file, and, when that fails too, drop it from the running
service (S4's LLR-murjr6): neither deletes a file or a keychain item.

From proposed 10 (the org-io half of org-node's control that deletes only
on a verified revocation or removal; the old plan wrote it as "implements"
of that org-node control, which org-io cannot cite: org-node exports no
item). In part 1 org-io itself deletes nothing: every deletion is
org-node's, returned on one of the two verified paths. Part 2 adds org-io's
own deletions (the store file, the keychain item and the retained
acknowledgement), each under the revocation-receipt requirement it mints,
and re-states this item's exception list by a dated note.

## The own-admin check, superseded (task T16a)

*Added 2026-10-08 by the owner's rulings D4 and D5 on the plan's §5
(PR-wk7zwq, and on-chain-client's report on the two-block read).* A change of meaning of a merged item, so a new ID
(hybrid rule).

**REQ-ntkjk6**: The software shall report whether this node is an admin of an
Organisation from one read, at one finalised block, of the account the
Organisation's H160 maps to and that account's proxy definitions: an admin
exactly when the account is mapped and a definition names, as delegate, the
node's own chain account (no co-signers configured) or the multisig account
of the node's own account and its configured co-signers at threshold 1, with
the runtime's `Any` proxy type and a delay of 0; "delegate that cannot
dispatch" when such a delegate is named only with another proxy type or a
non-zero delay; "not an admin" otherwise; and a failed read as an error,
never as "not an admin".
satisfies: derived
exported: yes
supersedes: REQ-f3eu9n

What it changes from REQ-f3eu9n: any delegate equal to the controller
counted, whatever its proxy type and delay, and the two values were read at
two blocks. The writer now forces the `Any` proxy type on every dispatch
through the proxy (on-chain-client's superseding call item), so "a definition
`(controller, Any, 0)` exists" is exactly "the chain will dispatch this
node's writes at once". The rule's first caller is part 2's limit-setting
command (owner ruling of 2026-10-07: S3b-io is the check's first caller).
