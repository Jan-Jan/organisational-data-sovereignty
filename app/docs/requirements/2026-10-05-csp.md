# Requirements — the webview's Content-Security-Policy

From owner ruling 4 of `docs/plans/2026-10-05-app-architecture.md` and the
hazard analysis in this change's `app/docs/risk/` draft (HAZ-e2zupz). The
requirement is realised in configuration, `app/src-tauri/tauri.conf.json`, and
verified by `app/src-tauri/tests/csp_policy.rs`.

---

**REQ-cj5jmx**: The software shall ship its webview with a
Content-Security-Policy in which `default-src` is exactly `'self'`; `script-src`
admits only `'self'` and hash or nonce sources, and so admits no
`'unsafe-inline'`, no `'unsafe-eval'` and no remote origin; `object-src`,
`base-uri`, `frame-ancestors` and `form-action` are each exactly `'none'`;
`connect-src` admits only Tauri IPC (`ipc:` and `http://ipc.localhost`); no
directive names any other network origin, wildcard or network scheme, and no
directive appears twice, whatever its case. The policy names no directive
except `default-src`, `script-src`, `style-src`, `img-src`, `connect-src`,
`object-src`, `base-uri`, `frame-ancestors` and `form-action`. The policy used by development
builds shall be configured separately, and shall differ from it only by adding the local development
server's origins (`http://localhost:5173`, `ws://localhost:5173`).
(implements: RC-mq2365)
satisfies: derived

Read "ships" as the `app.security.csp` value in `tauri.conf.json`, the policy
Tauri applies to the bundled build. Tauri appends hashes of the bundle's
inline scripts to `script-src` at build time. That is why hash sources are
admitted, and it is the part this requirement's test cannot see (the risk
draft's §3 records the limitation).

The rule against repeated directives exists because a browser applies the
first occurrence of a directive and ignores the rest. A policy that repeats
`script-src` would otherwise pass a check that read the later copy.

The directive allow-list exists for a similar reason (review round 1,
finding-1). Under CSP Level 3, `script-src-elem`, `script-src-attr` and
`worker-src` override `script-src` for the scripts they govern. A policy that
added `script-src-elem 'unsafe-inline'` would admit inline script while its
`script-src` still looked clean.
